Module 7 – Why Quantum Machine Learning (QML)?

Chapter 1: Limitations of Classical Machine Learning

1.1 Introduction

Machine Learning (ML) has transformed many industries including healthcare, finance, robotics, autonomous driving, cybersecurity, and recommendation systems. However, as datasets become larger and more complex, classical computers face several limitations.

Quantum Machine Learning (QML) aims to overcome some of these limitations by utilizing the principles of quantum mechanics.

1.2 What is Classical Machine Learning?

Classical ML uses algorithms running on classical computers to learn patterns from data.

General Workflow:

  2. Data Cleaning

  5. Prediction

  • Deep Learning

4.5 Important Note

Quantum parallelism does not mean all answers can be read out at once. Measurement yields one outcome, so quantum algorithms must use interference to increase the probability of correct answers.

Chapter 6: Current Challenges

6.1 Hardware Limitations

Today's quantum computers are in the NISQ (Noisy Intermediate-Scale Quantum) era.

Challenges include:

  • Limited qubits
  • Noise
  • Decoherence
  • Gate errors

6.2 Data Encoding

Classical data must be converted into quantum states.

Encoding methods include:

  • Basis Encoding
  • Angle Encoding
  • Amplitude Encoding
  • IQP Encoding

Data loading can become a computational bottleneck.

6.3 Error Correction

Large-scale fault-tolerant quantum computers require quantum error correction, which demands many physical qubits to create reliable logical qubits.

6.4 Algorithm Design

Many QML algorithms remain experimental.

Researchers continue improving:

  • Training methods

  • Generalization
  • Scalability

Overall Module Summary

Why Quantum Machine Learning?

Classical ML faces challenges with computation, optimization, and very high-dimensional data. Quantum computing introduces new computational tools such as superposition , entanglement , and quantum interference , which may provide advantages for certain machine learning tasks. However, current quantum hardware is still limited, and Quantum Machine Learning remains an active research area rather than a replacement for classical ML.

Key Takeaways

  • Classical ML struggles with scalability, computational cost, and high-dimensional datasets.
  • Computational complexity is a major factor in AI training and inference.
  • Quantum states naturally represent exponentially large state spaces.
  • Quantum parallelism enables new algorithmic approaches but does not automatically solve problems faster.
  • Quantum advantage is problem-specific and still under investigation for ML applications.
  • Current challenges include noisy hardware, data encoding, error correction, and algorithm development.
  • Hybrid quantum-classical systems are expected to play a significant role in the near future until large-scale fault-tolerant quantum computers become available.
