Chapter 1

Linear Algebra & Complex Hilbert Space

1.1 The Mathematical Language of Quantum Computing

Linear Algebra is the fundamental mathematical language of Quantum Computing and Quantum Information Science. Every physical quantum state is represented as a normalized state vector within a complex vector space called a Hilbert Space.

In classical computing, information resides in discrete binary registers ($0$ or $1$). In quantum computing, information resides in continuous complex vector spaces ($\mathcal{H} = \mathbb{C}^d$). The dimension of the Hilbert space scales exponentially with the number of qubits: $N$ qubits correspond to a $2^N$-dimensional Hilbert space.

ℹ️
Formal Definition of Hilbert Space $\mathcal{H}$ A Hilbert space $\mathcal{H}$ is a complete complex vector space equipped with an inner product that defines vector length, angles, norms, and orthogonality. For an $N$-qubit quantum system, the Hilbert space dimension is $d = 2^N$.
1. Scalars

In quantum computing, scalars are complex numbers ($c = a + ib \in \mathbb{C}$) containing both magnitude and phase angle. The complex phase enables wave interference.

2. State Vectors

An ordered column matrix of complex numbers representing the state of a quantum system. Single-qubit vectors have 2 elements; $N$-qubit vectors have $2^N$ elements.

3. Vector Norm (Length)

The norm or length of a vector $v$ is given by $\|v\| = \sqrt{\sum |v_i|^2}$. All valid quantum state vectors must be unit vectors with norm $\|v\| = 1$.

4. Inner Product

The inner product $\langle u|v angle$ measures state vector overlap. If $\langle u|v angle = 0$, the states are orthogonal and perfectly distinguishable.

5. Outer Product

The outer product $|u angle\langle v|$ multiplies a column vector by a row vector, creating a square matrix operator used for projections.

6. Hermitian Operators

A matrix $A$ is Hermitian if $A^\dagger = A$. In quantum mechanics, all physical observables (energy, spin, momentum) are represented by Hermitian operators.

7. Unitary Operators

A matrix $U$ is Unitary if $U^\dagger U = U U^\dagger = I$. All quantum logic gates are Unitary operators, preserving state norm and ensuring reversibility.

Complex Numbers & Quantum Amplitudes

2.1 Complex Amplitudes vs Classical Probabilities

Quantum mechanics replaces classical real-valued probabilities with complex probability amplitudes $lpha, eta \in \mathbb{C}$.

The complex nature of quantum amplitudes allows quantum algorithms to create Constructive Interference (amplifying correct answers) and Destructive Interference (canceling wrong solutions).

⚠️
Euler's Formula & Phase Representation $$z = r e^{i\phi} = r (\cos\phi + i\sin\phi)$$ Where $r = |z|$ is the magnitude and $\phi$ is the quantum phase angle. Phase shifts are essential for quantum algorithm acceleration.

Dirac Bra-Ket Notation & Operator Algebra

3.1 Dirac Notation Fundamentals

Invented by Paul Dirac, Bra-Ket notation is the standard mathematical notation for quantum states. A column state vector is represented as a Ket ($|\psi angle$). The conjugate transpose of a Ket is a row vector represented as a Bra ($\langle\psi|$).

📝
Computational Basis Representations $$|0 angle = egin{bmatrix} 1 \ 0 \end{bmatrix}, \quad |1 angle = egin{bmatrix} 0 \ 1 \end{bmatrix}$$ $$\langle 0| = egin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 \end{bmatrix}, \quad \langle 1| = egin{bmatrix} 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix}$$

Geometric Representation on the Bloch Sphere

6.1 Bloch Sphere Geometry

The Bloch Sphere is a 3D unit sphere that geometrically represents the state space of a single qubit. North Pole represents state $|0 angle$, South Pole represents state $|1 angle$, and points along the Equator represent equal superposition states.

Chapter 7

Quantum Measurement Postulate & Born's Rule

7.1 Projective Measurements

Quantum measurement extracts classical information from a quantum state. According to Born's Rule, measuring state $|\psi angle = lpha|0 angle + eta|1 angle$ yields outcome 0 with probability $|lpha|^2$ and outcome 1 with probability $|eta|^2$. Measurement irreversibly collapses the quantum superposition.

Python Code Hands-On: Qiskit 1.0 Circuit Simulation & Unitary Verification

Python 3.10 (Qiskit 1.0) 
import numpy as np
from qiskit import QuantumCircuit
from qiskit.quantum_info import Operator, Statevector

# Construct Bell State Circuit
qc = QuantumCircuit(2)
qc.h(0)
qc.cx(0, 1)

# Extract Unitary Matrix
U = Operator(qc)
print("Unitary Matrix Representation:")
print(np.real_if_close(U.data))

# Verify Unitary Property (U^\dagger U = I)
I_calc = np.dot(U.data.conj().T, U.data)
print("Unitary Verified (U^\dagger U == I):", np.allclose(I_calc, np.eye(4)))
