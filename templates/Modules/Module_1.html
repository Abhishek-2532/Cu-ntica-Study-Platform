Module 1 – Introduction to Artificial Intelligence

Chapter 1: What is Artificial Intelligence (AI)?

1.1 Definition of Artificial Intelligence

Artificial Intelligence (AI) is a branch of Computer Science that focuses on creating machines and software capable of performing tasks that normally require human intelligence.

These tasks include:

  • Learning
  • Reasoning
  • Problem Solving
  • Decision Making
  • Understanding Language
  • Recognizing Images
  • Planning
  • Creativity (Generative AI)

Simple Definition

Artificial Intelligence is the ability of a computer or machine to think, learn, and make decisions like a human.

1.2 Human Intelligence vs Artificial Intelligence

1.3 Why AI is Important

AI helps automate difficult and repetitive tasks.

Benefits include:

  • Faster decision-making
  • Reduced human errors
  • Works 24/7
  • Handles huge amounts of data
  • Improves productivity
  • Saves time
  • Saves cost

1.4 Characteristics of AI

An intelligent system should have:

  • Learning

    Ability to improve using experience.

    Example:

    • Netflix recommends better movies over time.
  • Reasoning

    Ability to make logical decisions.

    Example:

    • Chess-playing AI.
  • Problem Solving

    Finding the best solution among many possibilities.

    Example:

    • Google Maps finding shortest path.
  • Perception

    Understanding surroundings using sensors.

    Example:

    • Camera
    • Microphone
    • Radar
    • LIDAR
  • Language Understanding

    Understanding human language.

    Example:

    • ChatGPT
    • Siri
    • Alexa
  • Planning

    Making future decisions.

    Example:

    • Self-driving car planning next turn.

Humans have imagined intelligent machines for centuries.

Examples:

  • Mechanical dolls

2.2 Birth of AI (1956)

Artificial Intelligence officially began in 1956.

The Dartmouth Conference is considered the birth of AI.

Researchers included:

  • John McCarthy
  • Marvin Minsky
  • Claude Shannon
  • Allen Newell
  • Herbert Simon

John McCarthy coined the term:

Artificial Intelligence

Chapter 3: Types of Artificial Intelligence

AI can be classified in two major ways:

  • Based on Capability
  • Based on Functionality

3.1 Based on Capability

Narrow AI (Weak AI)

Designed for one specific task.

Examples:

  • Siri
  • Alexa
  • Face Recognition
  • ChatGPT (current systems are specialized despite broad abilities)

Advantages:

  • Fast
  • Accurate
  • Practical

Disadvantages:

  • Cannot perform all human tasks.

General AI (AGI)

Machine capable of performing any intellectual task that humans can perform.

Current Status:

  • Not yet achieved.

Super AI

Hypothetical.

Machine smarter than humans.

Exists only in theory.

3.2 Based on Functionality

Reactive Machines

No memory.

Only reacts.

Example:

  • IBM Deep Blue.

Limited Memory AI

Uses previous information.

Examples:

  • Self-driving cars
  • Recommendation systems

Theory of Mind AI

Would understand:

  • Emotions
  • Intentions
  • Beliefs

Still under research.

Self-Aware AI

Future concept.

Machine has consciousness.

Does not exist today.

Chapter 5: Future of Artificial Intelligence

5.1 Future Trends

The next decade of AI is expected to include:

  • More powerful Large Language Models
  • Better robotics
  • Autonomous transportation
  • AI-assisted scientific discovery
  • AI in personalized medicine
  • Human-AI collaboration
  • AI-powered education
  • Edge AI (AI running on local devices)
  • Multimodal AI (text, image, audio, video together)

5.2 Artificial General Intelligence (AGI)

Researchers aim to develop AI that can:

  • Learn any task
  • Adapt like humans
  • Solve unfamiliar problems
  • Transfer knowledge across domains

Challenges include safety, reliability, and alignment with human values.

5.3 AI in Quantum Computing

Quantum AI combines:

  • Quantum Computing
  • Machine Learning
  • Optimization

Potential applications:

  • Faster drug discovery
  • Financial optimization
  • Climate modeling
  • Cryptography
  • Quantum Machine Learning (QML)

5.4 Ethical Issues

Important concerns include:

Bias

AI may inherit biases from training data.

Privacy

AI systems require large amounts of personal data.

Transparency

Some AI models are difficult to interpret ("black boxes").

Job Displacement

Automation may replace certain repetitive jobs while creating new roles.

Security

AI can be misused for cyberattacks, misinformation, or deepfakes.

Accountability

Who is responsible when an AI system causes harm?

5.5 Responsible AI Principles

  • Fairness
